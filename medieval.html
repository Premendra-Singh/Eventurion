Medieval India

A Tapestry of Power and Culture

From the rise of the Delhi Sultanate to the grandeur of the Mughal Empire, explore a dynamic era of architectural brilliance, cultural synthesis, and transformative ideas that shaped the subcontinent.

Timeline of Medieval India 700 CE – 1800 CE

1

Harshavardhana &
Successor States

c. 606–750 CE

Harshavardhana united much of northern India under his rule, while regional powers such as the Chalukyas and Pallavas shaped the political and cultural landscape of the Deccan and South India.

2

Gurjara-Pratihara
Empire

c. 750–1036 CE

One of the dominant powers of early medieval northern India, the Gurjara-Pratiharas controlled vast territories and played a central role in the struggle for Kannauj.

View details →
3

Chola Empire

c. 850–1279 CE

The Chola Empire became a dominant power in southern India, renowned for its naval strength, overseas trade, grand temple architecture, and achievements in administration and art.

View details →
4

The Delhi Sultanate

c. 1206–1526 CE

The Delhi Sultanate was a succession of five ruling dynasties that controlled large parts of northern India for more than three centuries. It transformed political administration and contributed to development in Indo-Islamic architecture, art, and culture.

View details →
5

Vijayanagara
Empire

c. 1336–1646 CE

Founded in the 14th century in southern India, the Vijayanagara Empire grew into a major political and cultural power. Its capital, Hampi, became a centre of trade, religion, and architecture.

View details →
6

Rajputana Dynasties

c. 7th–18th century

Rajput kingdoms emerged as influential regional powers across northern and western India. Their rulers established fortified cities and kingdoms, participated in shifting political alliances and conflicts, and became closely associated with distinctive traditions of architecture, warfare, art, and courtly culture.

View details →
7

Mughal Empire

c. 1526–1857 CE

Founded by Babur in 1526, the Mughal Empire grew into a major power across the Indian subcontinent. Under rulers such as Akbar, Shah Jahan, and Aurangzeb, it shaped administration, architecture, art, literature, and culture.

View details →
8

Maratha Empire

c. 1674–1818 CE

Emerging under Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, the Marathas became a major power in western India. Their forts, military strategies, and administration helped expand Maratha influence across much of the subcontinent.
